Consulting

Find out what's worth automating — before anyone builds anything.

Most automation projects fail for the same reason: someone automated the wrong thing, or automated the whole process instead of the one annoying step inside it. Consulting is the work of finding that step.

You don't need a platform, a subscription, or a six-month transformation. You need someone to sit with how your business actually runs for an hour, and tell you honestly which parts are worth handing to a machine — and which parts aren't.

Sometimes the answer is a three-day build. Sometimes it's "your process is fine, fix the handoff instead." Both are useful answers. Only one of them gets sold to you by most agencies.

How it works

01

We walk your week

Not a pitch. You show what you and your team actually do — the inbox, the spreadsheet, the CRM nobody updates, the thing you re-type twice. Screen share is enough.

02

We map where time leaks

Each repeated task gets the same three questions: how often, how long, and does the system it lives in have an API. That last one decides what's cheap to fix and what isn't.

03

You get a written roadmap

What to automate first, what to leave alone, rough hours and cost for each, and what it saves. Yours to keep — whether you build it with us, in-house, or not at all.

What you walk away with

  • A ranked list of automation candidates, with the reasoning behind the order
  • An honest split between "worth doing now," "worth doing later," and "don't"
  • Estimated hours saved per week for each item — calculated from your numbers, not benchmarks
  • The integration reality check: what your current tools can and can't talk to

Where most businesses land

Starting out

Nothing automated yet. Usually the biggest wins are here and they're the simplest — one repeated task, one destination, done in days.

Partly there

Some tools connected, some Zaps running, nobody sure what still works. The job is usually consolidation, not more tools.

Advanced

Real systems already running. Here it's multi-step agents, self-healing workflows, and things that need a person who writes code.

Knowing which one you're in matters more than any specific tool. It's the first thing we figure out.
